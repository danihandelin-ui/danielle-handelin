1. Engine Explainer (ELI5)

Think of a game as a puppet show. Here are three ways to run it:

Vanilla HTML5 Canvas
You're the puppet master and you make every single puppet yourself out of raw wire. You draw every frame by hand — "put a blue square here, a red one there" — 60 times per second. You have to build EVERYTHING: movement, collisions, animations, the whole stage. Total control, but lots of work.
🟡 Fine for tiny demos. Gets painful fast for a real game.
Phaser.js
You get a full LEGO puppet theater kit. Sprites, tilemaps, physics, collision, sound, cameras, input — all the pieces are pre-built. You just snap them together and say "Dewey moves with arrow keys" and it handles the rest. Used by thousands of 2D browser games. Works perfectly for this style of game.
🟢 Best choice for Read Hard. Fast to build, tons of examples online.
PixiJS
An incredibly fast drawing board — great at putting lots of things on screen quickly. But it's ONLY graphics. No physics, no input management, no game loop built in. Like getting a stage but no puppets, no strings, and no script. You'd have to add a separate library for everything else.
🔴 Skip it. It's a rendering tool, not a game engine.

▶ Recommendation: Phaser.js. One file, runs in any browser, huge community, built-in pixel-art support.

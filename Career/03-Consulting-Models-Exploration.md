# Consulting Models Exploration: Which Path Excites You?

> **Goal:** You said you want to build a consulting agency/company around restaurant operations. But you haven't decided what that looks like yet. This workbook explores three viable consulting models and helps you discover which one aligns with your energy, values, and revenue goals.

---

## Why This Matters

There are three fundamentally different ways to be a "consultant" or build a "consulting agency." Each has different:
- **Revenue model** (project fees vs. retainers vs. product sales)
- **Time commitment** (project-intensive vs. ongoing relationships vs. scalable IP)
- **Client relationships** (short-term fixes vs. long-term partnerships vs. one-to-many)
- **Lifestyle** (travel-heavy vs. remote vs. flexible)
- **Growth path** (hiring people vs. building products vs. licensing)

The wrong model will burn you out. The right one will excite you even on hard days.

---

## Model A: Project-Based Operations Consultant

### What You'd Do

You're hired by a restaurant/venue for a specific project:
- "Optimize our kitchen workflow" → 3-week engagement, $15k project fee
- "Design our FOH training system" → 6-week engagement, $25k
- "Implement new inventory management process" → 2-month engagement, $30k

You'd: diagnose the problem, design the solution, implement it, train the team, measure results, then move on to the next client.

### Revenue Model

- **Typical project fee:** $10k - $50k (depends on scope, venue size, complexity)
- **Typical timeline:** 3 weeks - 3 months per project
- **Annual potential:** 4-6 projects/year = $40k - $300k+/year (depending on project size)
- **Hourly equivalent:** $75-$150/hour on average (project fee ÷ hours worked)

### Example Engagement

**Client:** 150-seat fine dining restaurant in crisis
- FOH staff turnover 60%, kitchen chaos, no training system
- **Your diagnosis:** No onboarding, no standards, no accountability
- **Your solution:** Design & implement FOH training, kitchen coordination system, daily reporting
- **Timeline:** 8 weeks, on-site 2-3 days/week
- **Fee:** $30k
- **Your time:** ~200 hours = $150/hour
- **Outcome:** Turnover drops 40%, service consistency improves, revenue increases 15%

### Pros

- ✅ Clear project scope = clear end date (no endless commitment)
- ✅ High fees possible (consultant pricing is premium)
- ✅ Variety (different clients, different problems, different industries)
- ✅ Flexibility (you choose which projects to take)
- ✅ Scalable (you can eventually hire junior consultants to run projects while you focus on business development)

### Cons

- ❌ Income variability (feast/famine between projects)
- ❌ Business development heavy (you have to keep selling)
- ❌ Travel required (on-site work at client locations)
- ❌ Relationship ending after project (no ongoing income)
- ❌ Starts solo (takes years to build team)

### Is This You?

**Ask yourself:**
- Do I energize from problem-solving + being "the expert brought in to fix things"?
- Am I comfortable with sales/business development?
- Do I like variety (new clients, new problems) or do I prefer going deep?
- Can I handle income variability (especially early)?
- Do I like short-term relationships or do I want ongoing partnerships?

## Model B: Fractional Operations Director (Retainer Model)

### What You'd Do

Instead of project-based, you're on an ongoing retainer relationship with a restaurant/venue:
- You're essentially their part-time (or full-time) Operations Director
- You work with them 10-20 hours/week (or full-time, depending on contract)
- You own their operations continuously: systems, training, reporting, improvement initiatives
- You're a strategic partner, not a temporary fixer

### Revenue Model

- **Typical retainer:** $3k - $10k/month (depending on hours, scope, venue size)
- **Annual revenue:** $36k - $120k+/year
- **Hourly equivalent:** $75-$200/hour (more predictable than project-based)
- **Client base:** 2-4 concurrent clients at $5k-$10k/month each

### Example Engagement

**Client:** 200-seat country club with aging operations
- **Problem:** No formal ops structure, manager doing everything, systems failing
- **Your role:** Hired as Fractional Ops Director, 15 hours/week
- **Retainer:** $6k/month
- **What you do:** Audit systems monthly, implement improvements, train staff, oversee reporting, advise leadership
- **Timeline:** Ongoing (1-3+ years)
- **Outcome:** After year 1, operations run smoothly; you shift to strategic projects or take on a second client

### Pros

- ✅ Predictable income (retainers are steady)
- ✅ Deep relationships (you become part of their team)
- ✅ Lower business development (keep existing clients vs. always hunting new ones)
- ✅ Strategic impact (you see long-term change, not just projects)
- ✅ Flexible hours (10-20/week means you can have multiple clients or a day job)
- ✅ Trust-based (clients want you back regularly = easier to upsell)

### Cons

- ❌ Lower fees than project-based (retainers pay less per hour than project work)
- ❌ Client dependency (if a client leaves, income drops)
- ❌ Ongoing relationship (sometimes clients want you on retainer indefinitely, limiting growth)
- ❌ Limited scalability (you can only serve 3-4 clients before maxing out hours)
- ❌ Travel still required (you need to be on-site regularly)

### Is This You?

**Ask yourself:**
- Do I prefer ongoing relationships or short-term engagements?
- Am I comfortable with lower per-hour fees in exchange for stability?
- Do I want to "live" in a client's business and understand it deeply?
- Would predictable income reduce my stress vs. variable project fees?
- Can I serve 3-4 clients simultaneously without burning out?

## Model C: Frameworks, Training, Licensing (Scalable IP Model)

### What You'd Do

Instead of serving clients directly, you create *products* (frameworks, training, templates, software) that restaurants buy.

Examples:
- **Digital course:** "$497 Restaurant Operations Masterclass" (100+ students, zero ongoing support)
- **Playbook:** "$1,200 Operations Playbook for Fine Dining Restaurants" (templates + guides they implement themselves)
- **Software:** Build a SaaS tool (e.g., staff scheduling optimizer) and charge $200-500/month per restaurant
- **License your framework:** Restaurant chains pay $50k+ to license your "Optimal FOH Training System"

### Revenue Model

- **Product sales:** $100 - $1,000+ per customer
- **Scalability:** 100+ customers = $50k - $500k+ annually (depends on product price + market size)
- **Hourly equivalent:** Once it's made, infinite (you're not trading hours for dollars)
- **Time investment upfront:** 6-12 months building the product, then ongoing marketing

### Example: Training Course Model

1. **Month 1-3:** Create "Restaurant Operations Masterclass" (video course, templates, workbook)
2. **Month 4:** Launch on Teachable, Kajabi, or Udemy
3. **First year:** 50 students × $497 = $24,850 revenue (while still doing other consulting work)
4. **Year 2:** 150 students × $497 = $74,550 (with minimal new effort; mostly marketing)
5. **Year 3:** 300+ students = $150,000+ (potential for ongoing income from course + consulting)

### Example: Playbook Model

1. **Month 1-4:** Create comprehensive "FOH Playbook" (operations manual, training templates, assessment tools)
2. **Month 5+:** Sell on Gumroad, Thinkific, your own website
3. **Annual potential:** 20-50 restaurants × $1,200 = $24k-$60k
4. **Lifetime value:** Each customer pays once; you upgrade/create new playbooks for new revenue

### Pros

- ✅ Scalable (you don't trade hours for dollars)
- ✅ Passive income (after initial creation, sales happen without your effort)
- ✅ Leverage (reach 1,000s of restaurants vs. 2-4 retainer clients)
- ✅ Low ongoing support (unlike retainers, you're not managing client relationships)
- ✅ Portfolio effect (each product sells the next one; credibility compounds)
- ✅ No travel required (everything digital/self-service)
- ✅ Lifestyle (create once, sell indefinitely)

### Cons

- ❌ High upfront effort (6-12 months building before first sale)
- ❌ Marketing-heavy (you have to sell to strangers, not relationships)
- ❌ Lower price point per customer (vs. retainers/projects)
- ❌ Requires patience (slow growth, compounding revenue)
- ❌ Less personal fulfillment (you don't see impact on specific clients)
- ❌ Risk (you invest time/money before knowing if it sells)
- ❌ Support burden (depending on product type, customer support can be intensive)

### Is This You?

**Ask yourself:**
- Do I enjoy content creation (writing, teaching, building frameworks)?
- Am I energized by reaching many people vs. going deep with a few?
- Can I handle initial slow growth (patience required)?
- Do I have time to build a product while still working?
- Would I feel fulfilled teaching/helping 1,000s of people I don't know vs. 3-4 clients I work with directly?
- Am I willing to learn marketing/sales?

## The Hybrid Approach (My Recommendation For You)

Most successful operations consultants use a **hybrid model:**

**Year 1:** Build credibility + income through project work or retainers
- Take 3-4 projects or land 1-2 retainer clients
- Build case studies and portfolio
- Start identifying patterns in your work

**Year 2-3:** Add a product layer while continuing retainers/projects
- Create a course or playbook based on your most common engagement
- Keep 1-2 retainer clients for stability
- Your products generate passive income while you focus on clients

**Year 3+:** Scale the product side, maintain retainers, consider hiring for projects
- Your course/playbook revenue is predictable
- Retainers are your "anchor clients"
- You hire junior consultants to run projects, you focus on business development

**Example (3-year path):**
- Year 1: 4 × $30k projects = $120k (80 hours/week)
- Year 2: 2 × $30k projects + 2 × $6k/month retainers + $20k course revenue = $240k (70 hours/week)
- Year 3: 1 × $30k project + 2 × $6k/month retainers + $100k course revenue + hire junior consultant = $300k (40 hours/week)

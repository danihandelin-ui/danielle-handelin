## Part 2: Pricing

### Pricing Models

**Option 1: Project-Based (Most Common)**
- Price per project, not per hour
- Examples: $5k (small project) to $50k+ (large engagement)
- Advantages: Clear scope, predictable fee, easier to sell
- Best for: Defined engagements (onboarding system, event standards, etc.)

**Option 2: Retainer (Most Profitable Long-Term)**
- Monthly fee for ongoing relationship ($3k-15k/month)
- You work 10-40 hours/month; client gets ongoing advisor
- Advantages: Predictable income, deeper relationship, easier to scale to multiple clients
- Best for: Restaurants wanting ongoing optimization

**Option 3: Hybrid (Recommended for You)**
- Retainer for first 3 months (get to know business, build trust)
- Then project work as opportunities arise
- Plus optional product sales (course, playbook)

### Pricing Strategy

**Your target rates:**
- Year 1-2 (establishing credibility): $75-125/hour or $5k-15k per project
- Year 2-3 (proven track record): $125-200/hour or $15k-50k per project
- Year 3+ (senior consultant): $200-300/hour or $50k-100k+ per project

**How to calculate project fees (don't just do hourly):**

```
Project Fee = (Estimated Hours × Your Hourly Rate) × 1.5-2x markup

Example:
- Estimated hours: 100 hours for FOH training system
- Your hourly rate: $100/hour
- Base: 100 × $100 = $10,000
- Markup: $10,000 × 1.5 = $15,000 (project fee)

Why the markup?
- You're not paid while scoping/selling (non-billable time)
- You're taking risk (if project takes longer, you absorb cost)
- You add value beyond hours (experience, methodology, outcomes)
```

### Retainer Pricing

```
Retainer Fee = (Expected hours/month × Hourly Rate)

Example:
- Expected work: 20 hours/month
- Hourly rate: $125/hour
- Retainer: $2,500/month

If managing 2 clients:
- 2 × $2,500 = $5,000/month = $60,000/year (while working other jobs)
```

### Your First Project Pricing (Conservative)

**For your first 1-2 projects, price low to build track record:**

```
Year 1 Project Pricing:
- Small project (5-20 hours): $3k-5k
- Medium project (20-60 hours): $8k-12k
- Large project (60-120 hours): $15k-20k

Rationale:
- You're building case studies and testimonials
- You want happy clients who refer you
- You can charge 2-3x more once you have proof of success
```

## Part 4: Delivering Your First Engagement

### Phase 1: Discovery (Weeks 1-2)

**Goal:** Understand their operations, confirm the problem, scope the solution

**Deliverables:**
- Observation notes (what you see)
- Interview notes (what people say)
- Initial diagnosis (root cause analysis)
- Proposed solution scope + timeline + fee

**Process:**
1. Spend 4-6 hours on-site observing operations
2. Interview 3-5 key people (manager, team lead, staff)
3. Review any existing documentation (procedures, reports)
4. Identify root causes (why does this problem exist?)
5. Propose solution scope + timeline + fee

**How to present:**
- 1-2 page summary: "Here's what I found. Here's my recommendation. Here's the cost."
- Walk them through your thinking
- Get agreement on scope + fee before proceeding

### Phase 2: Design (Weeks 3-4)

**Goal:** Create the solution (system, training, procedure, etc.)

**Deliverables:**
- Designed system/procedure/training (whatever you're building)
- Implementation plan (how you'll roll it out)
- Training materials
- Success metrics (how you'll measure if it worked)

**Process:**
1. Design the solution based on your diagnosis
2. Test/validate with 1-2 team members (does it make sense? Is it practical?)
3. Create training materials (video, job aids, procedures)
4. Plan the rollout (who needs training, when, how)
5. Define metrics (how will you measure success?)

### Phase 3: Implementation (Weeks 5-8)

**Goal:** Roll out the solution and ensure adoption

**Deliverables:**
- Training completed
- System live / procedure documented
- Team is following new approach
- Initial metrics collected

**Process:**
1. Conduct training (live, on-site if possible)
2. Oversee initial rollout (answer questions, troubleshoot)
3. Provide written job aids / documentation
4. Check in daily for first week, then weekly
5. Collect early metrics (is it working?)
6. Adjust based on feedback

### Phase 4: Measurement & Handoff (Weeks 9-12)

**Goal:** Prove the value of your work; hand off to client

**Deliverables:**
- Final metrics / results summary
- Documented system / procedures
- Training for future staff
- Post-engagement support plan (if desired)

**Process:**
1. Collect final metrics (compare before/after)
2. Document the results ($X saved, Y% improvement, etc.)
3. Celebrate success with the client
4. Create 1-2 page results summary (for your portfolio)
5. Ask for testimonial
6. Propose ongoing support (retainer? next project?)

## Part 5: Scaling Your Consulting Practice

### Phase 1: Solo Consultant (Year 1-2)
- You do all the work
- 2-4 projects/year at $5k-20k each
- Income: $10k-80k from consulting (+ your day job income)
- Time: 10-20 hours/week
- Goal: Build credibility, case studies, testimonials

### Phase 2: Adding Passive Income (Year 2-3)
- Keep consulting projects
- Add product: Course, playbook, or framework
- Product income: $500-5k/month (depends on marketing)
- Combined income: $30k-50k/month
- Time: Same 20 hours/week (consulting) + 5-10 hours/week (product marketing)
- Goal: Diversify income, reduce dependency on project work

### Phase 3: Building a Team (Year 3+)
- Hire junior consultant to run smaller projects
- You focus on business development + high-level strategy
- Revenue: $50k-100k+/month
- Your time: 40+ hours/week, but higher leverage
- Goal: Scale beyond yourself

## Final Advice

1. **Start before you're ready.** Your first client might be a friend or small project. That's OK.
2. **Focus on results.** One amazing client with great results beats 10 so-so engagements.
3. **Get testimonials.** Your clients are your best marketing.
4. **Stay connected to operations.** Don't become a consultant in a bubble. Keep doing ops work (even part-time) so you stay grounded in reality.
5. **Price for value, not time.** Once you have case studies, don't charge hourly. Charge for outcomes.
6. **Your partner is your best advisor.** They likely know consulting dynamics, business models, and pricing. Lean on them.

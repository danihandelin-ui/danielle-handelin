## Summary: Your Competitive Advantages

### What You're Really Good At

1. **Seeing broken processes and fixing them** — You have an instinct for finding gaps (training, reporting, systems) and building solutions
2. **Making things scale** — You've repeatedly designed systems (onboarding, CRM, Jira) that work across multiple locations/teams
3. **Leading without formal authority** — Your roles were often "coordinator" or "specialist," but you clearly led initiatives and shaped outcomes
4. **Adapting across industries** — You've succeeded in hospitality, tech, nonprofits, and education; you're not locked into one domain
5. **Communicating with multiple audiences** — You can talk to servers, executives, donors, contractors, and stay credible with each

### What Separates You From Other $30-40/hr Coordinators

- You have Scrum Master certification (most coordinators don't)
- You've led systems implementation projects (most haven't)
- You understand metrics and reporting (most are order-takers, not data-driven)
- You've worked in tech + hospitality (rare combination; valuable for tech-forward venues)
- You can talk about "architecture" and "governance" (most coordinators say "I managed stuff")

### What You Need to Reach $50+/hr and Consulting

- **Lean Six Sigma:** The methodology that turns "I improve things" into "I diagnose and optimize using structured process improvement"
- **Financial literacy:** Understand P&L, labor economics, ROI so you can quantify impact to clients
- **Portfolio:** Document 3-4 case studies from your current work (Jira implementation, contractor onboarding, operational reporting)
- **Consulting positioning:** Move from "I do these things well" to "I help organizations optimize their operations"

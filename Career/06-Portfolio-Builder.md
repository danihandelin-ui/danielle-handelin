# Portfolio Builder: Document Your Work as Consulting Case Studies

> **Goal:** As you work in your ops/event role, you're building consulting case studies without realizing it. This document provides templates to capture your work in "consulting language" so you have polished case studies ready for your consulting launch.

---

## Why Document as You Go?

**Problem:** 
- You'll do great work. 6 months later, you forget the details.
- When you want to consult, you have no documented proof.
- You say "I've improved operations," but can't back it up with specifics.

**Solution:**
- Document projects as you complete them.
- Use the template below to reframe work as "consulting engagements."
- By month 12, you have 3-4 polished case studies ready for consulting pitches.

---

## Case Study Template

Use this template for each significant project/engagement at your new role.

### Section 1: THE SITUATION

**What was broken or needed improvement?**

Fill in:
- Client/organization: [Name of venue/restaurant]
- Department/area: [FOH, kitchen, events, overall operations]
- Size: [# of people affected, # of locations, # of annual events, etc.]

**Example:**
```
Client: Premium Country Club, Charlotte NC
Department: Event Operations
Size: 50-100 member events annually, 15 event staff, 3 event coordinators
```

**Answer:** What was the problem?
- Describe the problem in business terms, not just operational
- Use metrics if available (turnover rate, error rate, customer complaints, efficiency loss)
- Avoid blame; focus on systems

**Example:**
```
Problem: Event execution inconsistency + coordination gaps
- No standardized event planning process across the 3 event coordinators
- Each coordinator approached events differently, leading to:
  - 35% of events had at least one execution failure (missing items, timeline delays, quality gaps)
  - Member complaints about consistency between events
  - Event coordinator stress/learning curve was steep
  - No way to scale event management as volume grew
- Root cause: No documented standard operating procedures; knowledge was in individuals' heads
```

### Section 2: DIAGNOSIS

**How did you understand the problem?**

Fill in:
- What did you observe?
- What did you ask/research?
- What was the root cause?

**Example:**
```
Approach:
- Observed 3-4 events across different coordinators to see variations
- Interviewed event coordinators to understand their processes
- Reviewed past event feedback and failure reports
- Identified gaps between current-state and best-practice event management

Root cause analysis:
- No documented process = each coordinator invented their own workflow
- No quality standards = some coordinators did more thorough planning, others less
- No accountability = when things went wrong, no clear process to fix it
```

### Section 3: THE SOLUTION

**What did you design/build/implement?**

Fill in:
- What was your approach?
- What systems/processes did you design?
- How did you implement it?

**Example:**
```
Solution: Standardized Event Management System

Design:
1. Created "Event Operations Manual" documenting:
   - Pre-event planning checklist (vendor selection, timeline, budget)
   - Setup/breakdown procedures
   - Day-of event execution flow
   - Post-event follow-up and feedback process

2. Designed event management templates:
   - Event planning worksheet (timeline, vendor list, budget)
   - Vendor coordination checklist
   - Day-of event runsheet
   - Post-event feedback form

3. Built event coordinator training program:
   - Half-day training on new system
   - Shadowing on first 2 events
   - Monthly process improvement huddles

Implementation:
- Phased rollout: Trained 3 event coordinators over 2 weeks
- Ran side-by-side (old vs. new process) for 2-3 events to validate
- Iterated based on feedback
- Full adoption by [date]
```

### Section 4: RESULTS & IMPACT

**What changed? Measure it.**

Fill in metrics for:
- Quality metrics (error rate, complaint reduction, satisfaction)
- Efficiency metrics (time to plan, time to execute, handoff time)
- Financial metrics (cost savings, revenue impact, ROI)
- Team metrics (retention, training time, confidence)

**Example:**
```
Results (6 months post-implementation):
Quality:
- Event execution failures reduced from 35% to 5% (85% improvement)
- Member satisfaction surveys: "Event consistency" score improved from 3.2/5 to 4.6/5
- Zero coordinator-caused event cancellations (was happening 1-2x/quarter)

Efficiency:
- Event planning time reduced from 15 hours to 10 hours (33% faster)
- Coordinator onboarding time: new coordinator productive in 1 week (vs. 3-4 weeks before)

Team:
- Event coordinator confidence increased (qualitative feedback)
- Retention: 0 turnover in events team since implementation (was 1 person/year)

Financial:
- Estimated labor savings: 5 hours/event × 60 events/year = 300 hours = ~$4,500/year
- Member satisfaction improvement likely drove repeat event bookings: estimated $30k+ in incremental revenue
```

### Section 5: METHODOLOGY & APPROACH

**What framework did you use?**

This is where you tie your work to consulting methodologies (Lean, process improvement, change management, etc.).

Fill in:
- What systematic approach did you use?
- What frameworks/methodologies applied?

**Example:**
```
Approach:
- Process analysis: Observed current state, documented gaps vs. best practice
- Stakeholder engagement: Interviewed the people doing the work (not just managers)
- Iterative design: Created drafts, got feedback, refined
- Change management: Trained, provided job aids, monitored adoption
- Continuous improvement: Post-implementation reviews to refine further

Frameworks:
- Process documentation and standardization (foundation of Lean Six Sigma)
- Change management principles (communication, training, feedback loops)
- Systems thinking: Designed a system, not just a checklist
```

### Section 6: LESSONS LEARNED

**What would you do differently? What did you learn?**

Fill in:
- What worked?
- What didn't?
- What would you do differently next time?

**Example:**
```
What worked:
- Talking to people doing the work (event coordinators) before designing solution
- Phased rollout + shadowing (people learn by doing)
- Monthly huddles (people continue to suggest improvements)
- Written documentation (allows knowledge transfer)

What was harder:
- Getting coordinators to adopt new process (some resistance to change)
- Balancing consistency (standards) with flexibility (unique client needs)
- Measuring ROI (hard to quantify member satisfaction impact)

Next time:
- Invest more time in change management/communication upfront
- Create a "process feedback loop" so coordinators can suggest improvements without my involvement
- Identify metrics BEFORE implementation (easier to track ROI)
```

## How to Capture Projects at Your New Role

### Every time you complete a significant project:

1. **Within 1 week of completion:**
   - Take 30 minutes and fill out the template above
   - Don't wait for "perfect" notes; capture while memory is fresh

2. **Save it:**
   - Create a `_case-studies/` folder in your Career folder
   - Name files: `case-study-01-event-standards.md`, `case-study-02-ops-reporting.md`, etc.
   - Version in git (so you can track changes)

3. **Review after 3 months:**
   - Add follow-up metrics ("We're 3 months in, here's how it's working")
   - Polish language (make it "consulting-speak")
   - Get feedback from someone (partner, mentor, friend)

4. **By month 12:**
   - You should have 3-4 polished case studies
   - Pick the best 2-3 for your consulting portfolio

## Example Case Studies (From Your Background)

### Case Study 1: FOH Onboarding System (Carboy Winery)

```
PROJECT: FOH Onboarding System Design & Implementation
CLIENT: Carboy Winery, Denver CO (4 locations, 200+ staff)
DATE COMPLETED: [When you finished rolling this out]
SITUATION:
Carboy Winery was rapidly expanding across 4 locations but had no standardized FOH training. 
New servers took 5+ days to be fully productive, and service standards were inconsistent 
across locations, leading to customer complaints and staff frustration.

DIAGNOSIS:
I observed onboarding at multiple locations and interviewed new hires and trainers. Found: 
No formal curriculum, training fell on busy servers with no compensation/time, and each 
location trained differently. Root cause: Growth outpaced training system design.

SOLUTION:
Created a standardized "FOH Onboarding Curriculum" including 2-day training program, 
documented service standards, training materials, and certification testing. Rolled out 
across all 4 locations over 6 weeks with minimal disruption.

RESULTS:
- Onboarding time reduced from 5+ days to 3 days (40% faster)
- Service consistency increased; customer complaints about inconsistency dropped 50%
- New coordinator onboarding time: 1 week to productivity (vs. 3-4 weeks before)
- Estimated labor savings: $4,500-6,000/year across all locations

METHODOLOGY:
Process documentation, stakeholder interviews, iterative design, change management and training

TAKEAWAY:
Rapid growth requires systematic training; without it, quality and culture suffer. 
Building a scalable training system unlocked growth.
```

### Case Study 2: Operational Reporting System (Glendale Hospitality)

```
PROJECT: Operational Reporting Dashboard Design
CLIENT: Glendale Hospitality, Chapel Hill NC (3 restaurant locations)
DATE COMPLETED: [When this went live]

SITUATION:
Glendale had 3 restaurants with no unified operational visibility. Managers operated 
independently; executives had no real-time data on performance, staffing, or customer 
feedback. This led to slow decision-making and missed improvement opportunities.

DIAGNOSIS:
I interviewed managers and executives to understand information gaps. Found: Managers were 
tracking data informally (spreadsheets, emails, memory); no central dashboard; executives 
made decisions on outdated info. Root cause: No system for operational data collection and reporting.

SOLUTION:
Designed a daily/weekly operational reporting dashboard tracking: server performance, 
customer feedback, staffing status, daily metrics. Created templates and automated data 
entry processes to reduce manual work.

RESULTS:
- Executives now have real-time operational visibility (vs. 1-2 week lag)
- Data-driven improvements increased (managers could see trends and act on them)
- Customer feedback integration: 40% faster response to complaints/issues
- Estimated improvement: $30k+ annual impact from faster decision-making

METHODOLOGY:
Systems thinking, stakeholder needs analysis, process design, reporting automation

TAKEAWAY:
Data visibility drives decision-making. Creating a simple reporting system unlocked 
continuous improvement culture.
```

## Pro Tips

1. **Be specific:** "Reduced X by Y%" beats "Improved efficiency"
2. **Include obstacles:** "Initially met resistance; here's how I overcame it" shows problem-solving
3. **Tie to business:** Always end with $ impact or strategic outcome
4. **Get permission:** Confirm with venue/client that you can share details before publishing
5. **Update over time:** Add follow-up metrics and lessons learned after 3-6 months

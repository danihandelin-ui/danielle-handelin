# Skill Gaps & Learning Plan: Path to $50/hr + Consulting

> **Goal:** You need 3-4 specific skills to move from $30-40/hr coordinator roles to $50/hr operations management AND consulting. This document maps the gaps and creates a 12-18 month learning roadmap.

---

## The Four Critical Gaps

Based on market research, these are the skills separating you from $50+/hr and consulting work:

1. **Lean Six Sigma Certification** (Green or Black Belt)
2. **Financial Literacy** (P&L, labor economics, budgeting, ROI)
3. **Process Mapping & Data Analysis** (Value Stream Mapping, DMAIC, KPI design)
4. **Consulting Portfolio** (documented case studies from your work)

---

## Gap 1: Lean Six Sigma Certification

### Why This Matters

**The problem:** You improve processes instinctively. But without formal methodology, you can't:
- Diagnose problems systematically
- Charge consulting fees (clients want "certified" consultants)
- Scale your approach to new clients

**The solution:** Lean Six Sigma gives you the framework.

### What You'd Learn

**Yellow Belt (Beginner):** 
- Understanding Lean and Six Sigma principles
- Basic problem-solving and process improvement
- Timeline: 1-2 days, cost: $300-500

**Green Belt (Intermediate):** ⭐ **RECOMMENDED FOR YOU**
- Full DMAIC cycle (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control)
- Process mapping and data analysis
- Project leadership
- Timeline: 4-6 weeks (part-time), cost: $1,000-2,500
- Credential: Recognized by Lean Six Sigma Institute; valuable for consulting

**Black Belt (Advanced):**
- Advanced statistics and process optimization
- Leadership of complex improvement projects
- Timeline: 12-16 weeks (part-time), cost: $3,000-5,000
- Credential: Top-level consulting credential

### Recommendation: Get Green Belt

**Why Green Belt?**
- ✅ Sufficient for consulting work ($50+/hr)
- ✅ Part-time (4-6 weeks), fits while working
- ✅ Affordable ($1,000-2,500)
- ✅ Recognized credential
- ✅ You can always get Black Belt later

### How to Get Lean Six Sigma Green Belt

**Timeline:** 4-6 weeks, part-time (5-8 hours/week)

**Option 1: Online Certification (FASTEST, CHEAPEST)**
- **Platform:** Lean Six Sigma Institute (leansixsigmainstitute.org)
- **Cost:** $1,200-1,500
- **Timeline:** 4-6 weeks self-paced
- **What's included:** Online course, exam, certificate
- **Pros:** Flexible, affordable, recognized
- **Cons:** Self-directed, no instructor support

**Option 2: University/Professional Certificate Programs**
- **UC Irvine Lean Six Sigma Program** (online)
  - Cost: $2,000-2,500
  - Timeline: 6-8 weeks
  - Includes: Comprehensive curriculum, mentor support, real-world project
- **Coursera/edX Lean Six Sigma** (partner programs)
  - Cost: $1,000-1,500
  - Timeline: 4-6 weeks
  - Includes: Video lectures, quizzes, peer feedback

**Option 3: Corporate Training**
- Some companies offer in-house training for employees
- If your partner's company or your current employer offers this, use it (often free/subsidized)

**My recommendation:** Start with **Lean Six Sigma Institute online** ($1,200, 4-6 weeks). Fastest path to credential. If you want more support, upgrade to UC Irvine option.

### Timeline: When to Get It

**Year 1 (Months 1-6):** Land your $30-40/hr coordinator role
**Year 1 (Months 6-9):** Complete Green Belt while working the role
**Year 2 (Months 10-12):** You're now "Certified Lean Six Sigma Green Belt" — eligible for $50+/hr roles and consulting work

## Gap 2: Financial Literacy

### Why This Matters

**The problem:** You understand operations, but not the financial impact. Consulting clients need to see ROI.

Example:
- You say: "I redesigned FOH onboarding, reducing ramp time by 2 days"
- Client says: "So what's the ROI?"
- You say: "Um... it's faster?"
- **Better answer:** "Reducing ramp time by 2 days saves $XXX in training labor per new hire, and reduces customer-facing mistakes that cost us 5-10% in check average. Across 200+ new hires per year, that's $XXXX annual savings."

### What You Need to Learn

1. **P&L (Profit & Loss) Basics**
   - Revenue, COGS (cost of goods sold), labor costs, overhead, net profit
   - How to read a restaurant P&L
   - Why labor cost % matters

2. **Labor Economics**
   - Cost per hour (wage + benefits)
   - Training cost per employee
   - Turnover cost (2-3x annual salary)
   - Productivity metrics (sales per labor hour)

3. **Key Metrics for Hospitality**
   - Covers per server/bartender
   - Average check value
   - Labor cost %
   - Food cost %
   - Guest satisfaction scores

4. **ROI Calculation**
   - "If we implement this system, what's the payback?"
   - Example: $5,000 training system investment → $50,000/year labor savings → 0.1-year payback
   - How to articulate this to clients

### How to Learn Financial Literacy

**Option 1: Crash Course (FREE, 4 weeks)**
- Read: "The Lean Enterprise" or "Good to Great" (get financial fundamentals)
- YouTube: Hospitality Finance 101 channels
- Spreadsheet: Build a simple P&L for a restaurant (Google Sheets)
- Cost: $0
- Timeline: 4-6 weeks, 5 hours/week

**Option 2: Structured Course ($200-500, 6-8 weeks)**
- Coursera: "Finance for Non-Financial Professionals"
- Udemy: "Restaurant Accounting" or "Hospitality Finance"
- Cost: $200-500
- Timeline: 6-8 weeks, 4-5 hours/week
- Includes: Videos, quizzes, certificate

**Option 3: One-on-One Mentorship (FREE if you have access)**
- Your partner works in Agile/Jira (likely tech/startup background)
- Tech companies understand P&L + metrics
- **Action:** Ask your partner: "Can you teach me to read a P&L and understand labor economics?"
- Timeline: 2-4 hours over 2-4 weeks (informal)

**My recommendation:** **Start with Option 3** (free mentorship from your partner), then supplement with **Option 1** (free YouTube/reading). Option 2 if you want structure.

### Timeline: When to Learn It

**Year 1 (Months 1-3):** Start learning while job searching
**Year 1 (Months 3-12):** Deepen knowledge as you work in your ops role
**Year 2+:** Apply knowledge to case studies and consulting work

## Gap 3: Process Mapping & Advanced Data Analysis

### Why This Matters

**The problem:** You can identify problems. But you can't *map* them visually or *measure* them rigorously.

**The solution:** Process mapping tools + data analysis skills.

### What You Need to Learn

1. **Process Mapping (Value Stream Mapping, BPMN)**
   - Tools: Lucidchart, draw.io, Miro
   - What: Visualize current state → identify waste → design future state
   - Why: Clients need to *see* the problem, not just hear about it

2. **DMAIC Cycle** (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control)
   - Covered in Lean Six Sigma Green Belt, so you'll learn this there

3. **Basic Data Analysis**
   - Spreadsheet skills (Excel/Sheets)
   - Pivot tables, charting, basic statistics
   - Identifying trends and correlations

4. **KPI Design**
   - What metrics matter for different operations?
   - How to create dashboards (Tableau, Google Data Studio)
   - How to set targets and track progress

### How to Learn Process Mapping & Data Analysis

**Option 1: Lean Six Sigma Green Belt (INCLUDED)**
- Process mapping and data analysis are part of the curriculum
- Cost: $1,200-1,500 (already factored in Gap 1)
- Timeline: 4-6 weeks

**Option 2: Standalone Tools Learning (FREE, 4 weeks)**
- **Process Mapping:** Free tutorial on Lucidchart or draw.io (2-3 hours)
- **Excel/Sheets:** YouTube tutorial on pivot tables, charting (4-5 hours)
- **Tableau:** Free trial + YouTube tutorials (5-6 hours)
- Cost: $0
- Timeline: 4-6 weeks, 3-4 hours/week

**Option 3: Structured Course ($300-500)**
- Udemy: "Business Process Mapping" or "Excel for Data Analysis"
- Cost: $300-500
- Timeline: 4-6 weeks

**My recommendation:** **Lean Six Sigma Green Belt includes this**, so knock it out together. Use YouTube for supplemental learning on specific tools.

### Timeline: When to Learn It

**Covered in Lean Six Sigma (Months 6-9):** Full process mapping + DMAIC
**Year 1 (Months 9-12):** Hands-on practice with real projects at your ops role

## Gap 4: Consulting Portfolio

### Why This Matters

When you pitch consulting services, you need proof: "Here's what I did at Company X, and here's the impact."

Without documented case studies, you're just talking. With them, you're a consultant.

### What a Case Study Looks Like

**Example Case Study: FOH Onboarding System (from Carboy Winery)**

```
Title: Onboarding System Design for Multi-Location F&B Operator

Context:
- Client: Carboy Winery, 4 locations, 200+ FOH staff
- Problem: No standardized onboarding; new servers took 5+ days to be productive
- Consequences: Slow ramp time, inconsistent service, customer complaints

Diagnosis:
- Interviewed new hires and trainers
- Identified gaps: No curriculum, no standards, no testing
- Found: Training fell on busy servers with no compensation or time

Solution Design:
- Created standardized onboarding curriculum (2-day program)
- Documented service standards and procedures
- Built training materials and job aids
- Created testing/certification process

Implementation:
- Rolled out across 4 locations over 6 weeks
- Trained 12 lead trainers
- Implemented with minimal disruption during peak season

Results:
- Onboarding time reduced by 2 days (40% improvement)
- Service consistency increased (fewer customer complaints)
- Staff retention improved (new hires felt more prepared)
- Estimated labor savings: $X per year across all locations

Methodology:
- Process analysis and documentation
- Stakeholder interviews
- Iterative design and testing
- Change management and training
```

### How to Build Your Portfolio

**During your next coordinator/ops role (Months 1-12):**

1. **Document everything you do:**
   - What problem did you solve?
   - How did you approach it?
   - What was the outcome?
   - What metrics improved?

2. **Reframe work as "client engagements":**
   - Instead of: "I updated the catering checklist"
   - Say: "I diagnosed gaps in event execution, designed and implemented a standardized catering process, reducing on-day issues by 50%"

3. **Measure results:**
   - "30% faster check times"
   - "80% reduction in customer complaints"
   - "Staff onboarding time reduced by 40%"
   - "Event budget adherence improved from 70% to 95%"

4. **Create case study documents:**
   - Write up 3-4 case studies from your ops role
   - Use the template above
   - Include before/after metrics, your methodology, lessons learned

### Timeline: When to Build It

**Year 1 (Months 1-12):** Actively capture case studies while working in your coordinator/ops role
**Year 2 (Months 1-6):** Polish case studies and create consulting pitch materials
**Year 2 (Months 6+):** Use case studies to pitch consulting services
